Extend fluency from majors to the full 78 (minors + courts, tracking the mentorship); internalize the *documented history* of tarot divination and permanently separate it from interpretive tradition; move 八字 from vernacular bridge to the first coherent classical system.

## Reading list — why each book, what to prepare, takeaways

### Rachel Pollack — minors & courts sections
- Track the mentorship card-by-card, same deepening-reference usage as Phase 1. Wen for court-card lookups.

### Decker/Depaulis/Dummett, *A Wicked Pack of Cards: The Origins of the Occult Tarot* (1996) — outside view, the phase's centerpiece
- **Why chosen over alternatives:** *not* Dummett's 1980 *The Game of Tarot* (600 dense pages, mostly game rules — optional Year 2). This is the revised, accessible treatment of exactly the occult-tarot origins that matter. Robert Place is the lighter bridge if this feels dry.
- **Prepare:** this is where scholarly and practitioner accounts diverge most sharply. Expect much practitioner literature you've read to be contradicted.
- **Takeaway — the Dummett thesis (memorize):** tarot invented in 15th-c. northern Italy as a *card game*; divinatory/esoteric use begins only in late-18th-c. France (Court de Gébelin 1781, Etteilla 1783–89). Implication: Golden Dawn correspondences (your codex) are a coherent ~1888+ interpretive overlay — master them as tradition, never mistake them for ancient fact.

### 《子平真诠》(沈孝瞻), with 徐乐吾《评注》 — 八字 inside view
- **Why chosen:** the best-structured entry among the 命理四书 — shortest, focused on 格局 method; the canonical "staircase" before 滴天髓.
- **Prepare (critical):** Chinese readers warn 徐乐吾's annotations mislead on 用神 ("误人子弟之处太多") — read annotations as one voice, not authority. Bring disputed 用神 determinations to the weekly teacher.
- **Takeaway:** an internally coherent 格局 system; the discipline of one framework before layering others.

### Papus, *Le Tarot des Bohémiens* (French original) — French track opens
- **Why French:** the A.P. Morton English translation is flawed; the French *replaces*, not adds. Read as a *primary source of the occult tradition* (the Egyptian-origin claims are documented-false per Dummett — that juxtaposition is the exercise).

### E.E. Evans-Pritchard, *Witchcraft, Oracles and Magic Among the Azande* (1937) — non-magic track
- **Why chosen:** foundational anthropology of divination — how oracles function socially, and how believers rationally handle oracle failure ("secondary elaboration").
- **Takeaway:** a lens for observing your own practice's social/psychological mechanics without debunking or believing.
