## Why (key decisions)

- **A new top-level directory, not a fit into an existing one.** `/sources/` is for verified primary texts — putting course material there would breach §1.2 by making non-primary material look citable. `/foundations/` is shared bedrock, Tier-2, and study notes are neither. `/log/` is session records. The study track is a genuinely different kind of object: input to the researcher's learning, not to the corpus. It gets its own space with its own (lighter) rules.
- **The brief already anticipates this track, without naming a home for it.** §7 names "the researcher's own training need … tied to her chart calculation course", and `/queue/` carries `slice1-train-001/002`. So this is filling a gap the brief left, not expanding scope. Those two training tasks still deliver into `foundations/calendar/` as originally specified — they are corpus-bound exercise sets, not study notes; I did not redirect them. If the researcher wants them moved under `/study/`, that is a Tier-2 call and the line to amend.
- **Three boundary rules, written into `/study/INDEX.md` because they touch the constitution:** (1) `/study/` is not `/sources/` — a course quoting 渊海子平 does not verify that quote; (2) the no-translation policy applies unchanged inside `/study/`; (3) study content earns its way into the corpus through a normal `intake:` session against a verified source — the corpus note is written fresh from the source, never lifted from the study note. Without rule 1 especially, this directory becomes the exact hole in §1.2 that the citation rule exists to close.
- **No structure pre-built under `/study/` beyond `inbox/`.** Subdividing by tradition/course/term before seeing the files would be guessing. The files decide.
- **Tier 2, on the designated branch.** New top-level directory + rules that reference the citation constitution = load-bearing. `/study/` leaf content (notes, drills, progress) is Tier 1 once the shape is approved.
